About Me

Real food, freshly milled grains, and slow fermentation—started for my own family and shared with our neighborhood.

I'm a father of two here in Boca Raton. This micro-bakery didn't start as a business plan—it started in my own kitchen because I wanted to feed my kids better bread and snacks than what I could find on grocery store shelves.

Even "artisan" or "whole wheat" loaves at the store are usually made with shelf-aged flour, fast commercial yeast, added sugar, and dough conditioners. I began milling whole wheat, rye, spelt, and einkorn berries fresh right before mixing, and relying strictly on slow sourdough fermentation. Freshly milled whole grains keep their intact germ oils, fiber, and Vitamin E, while long natural fermentation breaks down phytic acid so the grain's minerals are actually absorbable.

Everything I bake falls into two buckets. First, wholesome everyday staples that are almost impossible to buy with clean ingredients—hearty sourdough boules, real-ingredient sandwich bread, toasted granola, and low-sugar nut spreads. Second, traditional French and Finnish-inspired pastries, cakes, and brioches. Even when I'm making something indulgent, I refuse to take the ultra-refined shortcut: every sweet on my menu uses real butter, restrained sugar, and at least 10% freshly milled whole-grain flour.

Everything is baked in small batches to order—the exact same loaves and pastries that go on my own family's table.
